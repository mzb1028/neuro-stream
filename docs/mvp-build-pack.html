MVP Build Pack

Everything needed to build the bench prototype that proves the four risky claims: ±2% dosing, smart cartridges, ±2 °F water, and in-stream dissolving. One dispense station, hand-swapped cartridges, ~$1,150 in parts, about two weekends of assembly. Full details live in the repo: docs/mvp-build-spec.md.

01Electrical — every wire

Three voltage zones, color-coded. The mains zone (red) stays sealed in its own earthed metal box — and the whole heater section can be skipped at first: three of the four tests run on cold water.

02Plumbing — every tube

One line, five stops: tank → pump → flow meter → heater → mixing cone. All silicone tube, 8 mm OD. The single geometry that matters: the water inlet enters the cone off-center so the water spins.

03The cartridges — printed and sectioned

Print the housings in clear resin so you can watch the material move. The screw and pump parts print in nylon or machine from POM. Never print seals — buy silicone duckbill and umbrella valves.

Both cartridges end in the same 6-lobe socket, so one motor spins either. An NFC sticker on each carries its ingredient name and its measured grams-per-turn — the machine reads it on contact and refuses to dispense from a tag it can't read.

04Build order — risk first

Frame + dry dosing (no water)Tower frame, motor below the hanging cartridge, glass on the load cell. Command turns, weigh powder. This alone proves the ±2% claim.
Cold water loopTank, pump, flow meter, mixing cone. Food-dye the water to see the swirl film, then run dissolution tests at room temperature.
Smart tagsNFC stickers on the cartridges; write each one's measured dose-per-turn; firmware reads before dosing.
Heat — last, safety chain firstGFCI, earthed mains box, two thermal cutoffs, relay, watchdog, boil-dry guard — then tune temperature control to ±2 °F. Not comfortable with mains wiring? Hire an appliance tech for a day, or stay cold — 3 of 4 claims need no heat.
